**Game 2 adapting arena and robustness, version 1**

Preserve all earlier Game 1/2 studies, human literals, and checkpoints. Treat the
first Game 2 human/population outcomes and retrospective zero-five probes as known
development evidence. This stage studies adaptation and expands search coverage.
It does not infer applicant shares, certify Nash convergence, or select a final
Game 2 submission. The Game 1 recommendation remains (3,8,4,44,41).

Establish an exact response oracle for one specified opponent triple. At each castle
only below/equal/above the maximum opponent bid matters. Enumerate the at most 3^5
cells, reject cells whose bid intervals cannot sum to 100, and score a legal cell
representative exactly. This is global for a fixed triple, not for its population.
Audit on exhaustive reduced games. Independently scan every default legal allocation
against the fixed triple consisting of all-in castles 3,4,5. Its best possible rank
payout is one. Along with the previously proved all-in-five worst-group floor of
one, this caps the best worst-group guarantee, even for a randomized focal entry,
when three opponents can choose that coordinated fixed triple. It is not a claim
about iid applicant-population minimax value or a Nash payoff.

Start every training arena from the previous 47-vector shortlist, all 13 anchors,
posthoc zero-five variants, known counters and five all-in forts, plus 64 separately
seeded unrestricted vectors. Keep every action. Retain the complete 2,593-vector
old candidate archive as an innovation source without forcing it into the active
pool. All five castle bids may range from 0 through 100 subject to the budget.

Run eight primary paths with the official rank-payout objective. Cross replicator
and payoff-replacement revision, zero/25% historical-opponent mass, and balanced
versus heavy-five initial conditions. Use 0.5% mutation. Run four additional paths
with fixed objective-type shares 60% rank payout, 20% castle points, 20% first-place
credit, crossing the two rules and zero/1% mutation. These are sensitivity models;
fitness units cannot change type proportions. Mixed paths have 25% historical
opponent mass and a sparse initial condition. Report the groups separately.

Each path runs 240 steps. Every fitness estimate uses 1,024 fresh shared opponent
triples from the current/historical mixture. Numerical dynamics use approximate
Monte Carlo fitness, and are not an exact replicator trajectory. Every 20 steps
through step 200, search a finite field containing active strategies, prior-archive
samples, large and threshold-crossing mutations, new unrestricted vectors, and
exact responses to sampled triples and the most prevalent allocation's clones.
Fit responses to current and time-average populations on 2,048 shared triples.
Use a separate 8,192-triple gate for each rank-response/incumbent comparison.
Report fixed-sample paired bounds with a declared maximum of 240 gates. Gates do
not establish a global best response; all response admissions follow their stated
source rule even if improvement is unresolved. Admit selected rank responses,
active wrong-objective responses in the mixed paths, a clone counter, and a large
mutation, deduplicating each wave. New or rediscovered actions receive seed mass;
reseeding allows counters with zero numerical share to return. Retain old support.

The final 40 steps admit no new actions. Save final, whole-run average, and last-40
average populations, their type shares, class shares, checkpoints, RNG state,
innovation targets, search fields and independent gates. Mutation remains as
specified. A settling period does not prove convergence. Provide interrupted-run
recovery with content/source checks.

Use the union of the old field, initial pool, and all admitted actions as a finite
post-training candidate field. On independent selection triples from the twelve
last-40 distributions and four known static references, predeclare four selection
rules: primary-rank tail mean; primary-rank tail minimum; minimax regret across all
sixteen empirical selection scenarios; half static-center mean plus half primary
rank-tail mean. All regret benchmarks are restricted to this finite field. Selection
is approximate/statistical, not a complete-action Game 2 oracle.

Keep the three human literals, initial low-five and zero-five fits, zero-five probe,
equal spread, and Game 1 allocation as fixed comparisons. After fitting, freeze all
new primary choices and the evaluation shortlist before new evaluation sampling.
Fresh triples on the 36 trained arena distributions test score estimation, not
population generalization. Also generate eight new broad static scenarios after
the freeze with rotated priorities, unrestricted first-four bids, middle-five bids,
and counter-rich clouds. Their definitions and seeds are fixed before training.
Use 32,768 shared evaluation triples per model. Keep arena versus new-static
results separate and do not interpret scenario counts as applicant frequency.

Report candidate scores, finite-field response gaps, confidence bounds, final and
average class shares, and the strongest discovered counters. No final snapshot or
large population share is called a universal winner. Verify the exact group oracle,
all 4,598,126 allocations for the worst-group cap, new metric scorer against the
foundation, complete deterministic path replay, checkpoint integrity, selection
accounting, independent gates, legal joint draws, holdout freeze, score sums, and
unchanged earlier checkpoints. Further fitting to these evaluation results will
require a subsequent fresh challenge before final selection.
